The Brain / Use cases / Order to cash

Use case · finance and operations

The order was fine.
Getting paid for it took nineteen days.

Order raised, dispatched, invoiced, matched, collected. Between each pair of stages a person carries a number from one screen to another.

What changes on Monday: the carrying, the comparing and the chasing stop being done by hand.

Book a Diagnostic Sprint The other three workflows See it running first

The same month, with one memory

The joins stop being people

Day 1, 08:20

The order is read once, into one record

A mail, a portal, a PDF. Whatever arrived becomes one record with the lines, the price and the original attached.

Day 1, 08:22 · a person confirms

Creating the order waits for a yes

The record is shown before it exists. The operator corrects one quantity and confirms.

Day 2, 17:40

A short delivery is raised the day it happens

Dispatch is attached to the order line, so a short line reaches the site manager the same day.

Day 3, 09:00 · a person confirms

The invoice is drafted, then approved

The draft carries the customer’s own purchase order reference, taken from the order document rather than typed. A person clicks.

Day 3, 09:01

The three-way match runs on every invoice

Order, delivery note and invoice, line by line. Where they agree, nobody spends a minute. Where not, a person reads a short queue.

Day 34, 07:20 · a person confirms

The chase is drafted with the evidence in it

An invoice past terms is surfaced with its order, delivery and reference. Finance clicks send.

What had to be true for this to work

Four mechanisms, not four features

THE CONNECTED SYSTEMS

Your systems, as they are

Nothing is replaced.

THE FOLDER

One container per customer

Order, delivery note, invoice and thread in one folder.

THE CONFIRM GATE

No invoice, no reminder, without a click

71 of the 287 actions wait for a person.

THE INTERRUPTION BUDGET

A queue short enough to read

At most 40 interruptions per organization per hour and 5 per person.

What this does not do

Three things it will not do for you

It does not decide credit. That stays with the person who carries the risk.

It does not issue an invoice or send a reminder on its own.

It does not make a disagreement disappear.
