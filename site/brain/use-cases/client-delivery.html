Use case · delivery

The brief is signed.
Nobody knows what is blocking it.

One engagement across a project tool, chat, mail, documents and a calendar. Four hold a piece of the answer and none holds the answer, so somebody opens five tabs before every review.

What changes on Monday: the engagement arrives as one item, with what is blocked and why, and the chase drafted.

The same week, with one memory

The same five systems. One picture.

Monday 09:10

The client arrives as one thing, not five

The brief, the deck, the open tasks, the thread and the next two meetings, together because one folder holds this client.

Monday 09:11

Blocked items carry the reason

Two tasks are blocked, and each names why: five unresolved comments waiting on the client.

Monday 09:13

Nine days of silence becomes a signal

Absence is judged like a change: against the role, the surrounding work and whether it is worth interrupting anyone now.

Monday 09:14

The chase is drafted, in the thread it belongs to

A reply names the five open comments, the sign-off and the two dates that move if it slips. Not sent.

Monday 09:16 · a person confirms

The account lead edits one line and clicks send

Sending is one of the 71 actions that stop for a human confirmation. Approval counts only from a door where a person clicked.

Thursday 10:35 · a person confirms

What was decided in the room is written back

Two dates move, one task is reassigned, a follow-up is booked. Each write is shown before it runs and approved together.

What this does not do

Three things it will not do for you

It does not decide whether to keep a client, reduce a scope or escalate.

It does not send anything on its own.

It does not invent context that was never written down.
