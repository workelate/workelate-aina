The Brain / Architecture

For the architect who will be asked to sign

One loop, a small vocabulary,
and two checks on every claim.

This is the page for the person who has to defend the decision internally. The Brain is a single decision loop with no business branches in it, 15 verbs that resolve to concrete actions in the systems you connect, a confirmation gate in front of anything destructive, and two checks between what the system did and what it may tell you it did. Where a claim cannot be proved, it is marked unproved rather than dressed up.

01 · The bet

One loop, not a rules engine

Every signal takes the same path: sense, recall, judge, act with confirmation where the action is destructive, prove what was done before saying so, remember the outcome.

01

No business outcome is decided by a branch somebody wrote

The policy lives in your documents, read at decision time. The only hardcoded logic is reflexive: confirm, fence, rate limit, verify.

02

Why that is the whole bet

One rule per scenario is brittle forever. One reasoner over your documents means the same signal can be decided differently for two clients whose contracts differ.

03

The twelfth application test

A new application declares its own events and nouns, and the Brain reads that declaration. A condition inside the Brain naming an application is a defect.

02 · Sense, recall, judge

Context is assembled before an opinion is formed

Sense

Applications declare their own events

No polling, no screen scraping. What a connected system emits is what the Brain can see.

Recall

A work graph, and memory that names its owner

Deals, threads, tickets, documents and people are linked, every stored fact names its organization, and a question about one client reads that client.

Judge

Silence is the default outcome

Each change is scored against the person, read against the surrounding work, then put to the model once. At most 40 interruptions per organization and 5 per person per hour by default.

03 · Act, prove, isolate

A gate in front of the dangerous ones

15

Verbs on the wire to the model. That number only ever falls.

287

Concrete actions those verbs resolve to.

190

Business objects addressed across the Brain’s own 12 surfaces.

71

Actions classified destructive, each waiting for a human yes.

01

A model cannot approve its own action

Approval is recognised only from a door where a human clicked. Delete and send are gated by category, so a new action under either is gated from the moment it exists.

02

Doing and saying are two different events

A write is re-read inside your organization’s fence before it is believed, then the drafted sentence is compared against what the tools returned. Both fail open and abstain when uncertain.

03

Identity comes from the session, never from the model

Identity and token fields are stripped from model output before an action runs. Where the tenant fence cannot be applied, no query runs and the answer is unknown, never a pass.

04 · What is not claimed

The limits, stated before you find them

01

No certification is claimed

If procurement requires one, it is not in hand today.

02

There is no connector catalogue number

The connector set is whatever the engagement connects. The Sprint connects one system and shows you the result first.

03

Counts describe the product, not your deployment

They prove what the Brain can reach, not that every action has been exercised against your systems. The full list is on the proof page.
