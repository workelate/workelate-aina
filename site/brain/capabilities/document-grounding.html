The Brain / Capabilities / Document grounding

RECALL

Your policy already exists.
It is in the contract, and it is read at decision time.

Every automation project reaches the same fork: translate the agreement into rules that rot the day it is amended, or read the agreement itself every time a decision is made. The Brain reads the statement of work, the terms and your own written policy at decision time, as evidence.

What changes on Monday: amend the clause and the next decision is made against the new clause. No reconfiguration, no window running last quarter’s agreement.

What it looks like on a Tuesday

The same request, two clients, two opposite right answers

10:05

Client A asks for one more page. Yes, do it

Crosses mail and documents. A retainer with forty percent of the monthly allowance unused, in a clause read on this turn. The page is inside what has been paid for.

10:07

Client B asks for one more page. Raise a change note

Crosses mail, documents and the project system. Fixed scope, with a clause requiring written approval outside the deliverable list. The same sentence, a different contract, the opposite correct action.

10:12

Both answers quote the clause, and one waits for a click

Crosses documents and mail. A citation pointing at nothing is stripped before anyone sees it. Sending the change note is one of the 71 confirm-gated actions, so a person decides.

How it works

The argument against a rules engine, made mechanically

01

No branch in our code decides a business outcome

Nothing reads the client name and picks an answer. If it did, a thousandth client would need a thousandth branch. Reasoning over retrieved text serves cases nobody enumerated.

02

The opposite-answers pair is a test, not an anecdote

The stored evaluation cases include matched pairs where the same event must produce different outcomes because the documents differ. A rules engine cannot pass both halves.

03

What is claimed afterwards is checked against the data

A cheap tenancy-fenced re-read proves the record it says it wrote exists, and the sentence written is compared to what the tools returned. Both fail open, so they only downgrade a claim they can refute.

What it is not

Three honest limits

It is not legal advice. It reads what your documents say and reasons like a capable account lead.

It cannot ground a decision in a document you never gave it.

It does not make a badly drafted agreement coherent. Where two clauses contradict, it says so.
