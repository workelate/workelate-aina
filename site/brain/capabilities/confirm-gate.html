The Brain / Capabilities / The confirm gate

ACT

A model cannot approve its own action.
Not because we told it not to.

71 of the 287 actions the Brain can take are classified destructive: send, delete, share outward, anything that lands on a person rather than a row. They stop for a human yes, and a yes counts only when it came from a door where a person clicked.

What changes on Monday: work runs up to the last inch and stops in front of you, one click from finished. The worst case of a wrong judgment is a draft somebody declined.

What it looks like on a Tuesday

A chase that got written, waited, and then got sent

09:12

The silence is judged worth a person

Nine days without a reply on a thread carrying an overdue sign-off. Nothing is sent. The item only clears the interruption budget: 40 per organization and 5 per person an hour.

09:13

The reply is composed, and held

Recipient, subject, body, the five open comments named, the two dates that move if this slips. A complete message, and a draft, because send is one of the 71.

09:16 · a person confirms

The lead edits one line and clicks

The click happens in the product, under her own session. Consent is minted there, and only then does the send run. Under four minutes, and the tone was hers.

How it works

Three mechanisms, and the second is the whole argument

01

Destructive is a property of the action, not a mood

Anything that deletes and anything that sends is gated by being that verb. A new action of that shape arrives gated, not open and waiting for a list to be updated.

02

Consent is minted at a door, never read off model output

Approval is accepted from one place: a request from a surface where a person clicked, carrying their verified session. A tool cannot read its own permission off the text that asked for it.

03

The yes belongs to somebody

Identity comes from the session, never from the model. Anything the model produces that names an identity is discarded before an action runs, so every approval is attributable.

What it is not

Three honest limits

It is not a review of whether the action was a good idea. It proves a person said yes, nothing about the decision behind it.

It is not protection against clicking without reading. A confirmation that becomes a reflex has stopped working, which is why it is 71 actions, not all 287.

Reading is not gated. What a session may read, and whose click counts for each workflow, is settled in the engagement.
