The Brain / Questions

The short answers

Twelve questions,
answered without the sales gloss.

These are the questions we are actually asked, in the order they usually arrive: what this thing is, what it does when nobody is talking to it, what it is allowed to change, where your data sits, what it costs, and what happens the day it is wrong. Several answers concede a limit. Those are the ones worth reading, because the limits are what you will hit in week three whether or not we printed them here.

01 · What it is

What it is

What is the Brain, exactly?

WorkElate Chief, our own product, installed on top of the systems you already run. It holds one memory of the work crossing those systems, judges what is worth a person’s attention, and carries work forward with a human yes in front of anything it changes.

Is this a new application my people have to learn?

No. It does not become a system of record, so nothing is migrated and nobody lives in it. People meet a ranked list in the morning and a confirmation request when something wants to change.

02 · What it does

What it does, and what it can change

What happens when nobody types anything into it?

It watches. Each change is scored against a person, checked against the surrounding work, then judged once for whether it is worth saying now. What survives reaches you as a ranked briefing with the reason attached.

How many times a day will it interrupt someone?

At most 40 per organization per hour and 5 per person per hour by default, with cooldowns. Those are preferences you change by saying so in plain English.

What can it actually do, as opposed to say?

15 verbs, not hundreds of buttons, resolving to 287 concrete actions across the Brain’s own 12 application surfaces and 190 business objects. Which of your systems they reach is what the Sprint establishes.

Can it send an email or delete something on its own?

No. 71 of the 287 actions are classified destructive and stop for a human yes. Approval only counts from a door where a person clicked, so a model cannot approve its own action.

03 · Data

Where our data goes

Where does our data sit?

The Brain reads what your systems emit and writes back only into them. It keeps the graph of links and the memory, and where that sits and how long it is kept are written into the engagement. See the security page.

Is our data used to train anybody else’s model?

No, and that is a written term in the engagement rather than a badge on a page. What it learns stays inside your own organization.

Do you have SOC 2, ISO 27001 or an equivalent?

No. We claim no certification of any kind. If procurement requires one before a pilot, that is a real gap, better found in the first conversation than in week six.

04 · Cost, and being wrong

What it costs, and what happens when it is wrong

What does it cost?

The Sprint is two weeks at a fixed fee, quoted before it starts. Builds are fixed price, $30K–$100K, and scope creep is our problem. The pricing page says what is not priced publicly.

What happens when it is wrong?

It will be, sometimes. Because destructive actions wait for a human yes, a wrong judgment costs one declined suggestion rather than a sent email. That decline is a signal the next judgment uses.

What if we run it for eight weeks and decide it is not working?

You stop, and keep the code, the data and the system. Nothing was decommissioned, so there is nothing to restore. The downside is one team’s eight weeks.
