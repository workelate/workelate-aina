Case studies / RockProsUSA

One system. Thirteen quarries.
Twelve months of receipts.

RockProsUSA is an aggregates producer running 13 sites in the United States. Dispatch, invoicing and daily reporting moved onto agents in one build. The numbers on this page are their first twelve months on the system, and every one of them is an operating number, not a satisfaction score.

What we actually built

Five surfaces, one dispatch spine.

This was not a plug-in bolted to an ERP. We built the whole surface an aggregates operation touches, and the engine underneath it, so that a truck movement, an invoice line and a production number all describe the same event.

01

Customer ordering app

Where the order is raised, so that the first record of a haul is created once, by the person who wants the material, rather than re-typed by three people downstream.

02

Trucker app

The hauler's surface: what has been offered, what has been accepted, what has been confirmed.

03

Driver app

Built native with React Native, because the field is the one place a web tab is not good enough: a driver at the pit with a weak signal is the hardest user on the system, and the whole cycle record depends on him.

04

Admin console

Where 13 sites become one board: who has trucks, who has orders, and which cycles broke pattern today.

05

The dispatch backend

The engine behind all four: order to invoice in one path, so the three-way match at the end is reading the same records the assignment at the start was made from.

Why the numbers moved

Three mechanisms, not three miracles.

01

The assignment carries its reasoning

Every proposed truck assignment arrives with the reasoning that produced it, which is what makes a human approval fast instead of an investigation. That is where the 38% lives: not in trucks driving quicker, but in the minutes between an order existing and a truck being confirmed against it.

02

The match happens on delivery, not at month end

Weighbridge ticket, delivery note and purchase order reconcile as the haul completes. 2,140 invoices went out with zero manual touches because there was nothing left to reconstruct, only exceptions to look at.

03

The report is a by-product, not a task

The daily report is assembled from the same records dispatch and invoicing already wrote, so it is finished by 6:00 AM with no human in the loop. Hours that used to go into assembling numbers are a large share of the 11,200 returned.
