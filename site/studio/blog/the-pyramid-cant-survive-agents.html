The consulting pyramid
can't survive agents

Book a call

Every large-firm quote you have received was priced by a pyramid: a partner at the top, managers in the middle, a wide base of junior billable hours. The model was rational: enterprise software needed volume labour. Juniors wrote the boilerplate, ran the tests and built the decks, and the margin on their hours funded the firm.

So the quote was never about your outcome. For the firm, your project is a revenue line: how many people billed for how many months. For you, the pitch is cost saving: a better rate card. One sells headcount, the other buys a discount, and the system is a byproduct.

The pyramid is priced on effort, so it needs more effort. Fix the price to the outcome, and effort becomes our problem instead of your invoice.

That base, the volume layer, is what AI now does: code, test suites, data plumbing, documentation, report assembly. Not the judgment. When the base becomes software, what is left is senior judgment, and senior judgment does not need forty people and eighteen months.

The giants are not dying; a forty-country core-banking migration still needs an army. But if you run a 200 to 2,000 person company, you were never the client that model was built for, only the one it was priced for. Mid-tier firms run the same pyramid with a thinner partner layer and a cheaper base.

The equation, changed

WE_AINA runs the opposite arithmetic. The price is fixed to the build, so we make money by shipping faster, not staffing longer. A senior team does the judgment, the Brain does the volume, a person approves every consequential action, and you own the code at the end.

When you next buy a build, ask three questions. Who does the work, by name? What can you check while it happens, a status deck or the operating numbers? And is the price a function of your outcome, or of how many people the model needs to bill? We answer with one senior team, one fixed number, one working system. Here is how it works.
